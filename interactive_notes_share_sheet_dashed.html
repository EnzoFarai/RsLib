External Fertilisation

External fertilisation is a biological process where the sperm fertilises the egg outside the body of the female, usually in water. This method is common among aquatic animals, such as fish, amphibians, and some invertebrates.

The Process

In this process, the female releases her eggs into the surrounding environment, and the male releases his sperm over them. The water acts as a medium for the sperm to swim to the eggs and fuse with them.

Environmental Requirements

Because the gametes are released into the open environment, water is absolutely essential. It prevents the eggs and sperm from drying out and provides a fluid medium that allows the sperm to swim actively toward the eggs. Without water, external fertilisation cannot occur.

Advantages and Disadvantages

External fertilisation typically involves the production of a very large number of gametes to increase the chances of successful fertilisation, as many eggs and sperm are lost to predators or environmental factors. However, it requires less energy for mating and parental care compared to internal fertilisation.
